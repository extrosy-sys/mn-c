Privacy Policy — NetHealth

App published by Extrosy · Last updated: 2 October 2026

The short version: NetHealth has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and Extrosy runs no servers — so we receive nothing from you. To test your connection the app has to contact public test servers, which see your internet address the way any website does. Your results, reports and logs stay on your device unless you choose to share them.

What we collect

Nothing. NetHealth does not send any information to Extrosy. There is no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no third-party SDK in the app — it contains only Google's standard Android libraries (AndroidX and Material Components).

What the app sends over the internet, and to whom

A network health checker cannot work offline: to measure your connection it has to talk to servers on the internet. Every server it contacts sees your device's public internet address and the request itself, exactly as any website you visit does; what those services do with that is covered by their own privacy policies. NetHealth sends them no personal information beyond what the test needs, as listed here.

Tools you run by handOnly what you type: the host you ping, trace, scan or look up. Your routerUPnP / NAT-PMP requests to the router on your own network. The router check adds a test port opening for two minutes and removes it; the "Two devices" waiting side asks the router to open its port for the session and closes it afterwards.

The connection monitorWhile you run it: a ping a second to your ping target, a kept-open HTTPS connection to www.google.com, a STUN request to Google every few seconds, DNS look-ups and the sign-in page test - the items you can switch off are listed on the Monitor screen.
Devices on this networkOnly your own local network: pings, connection attempts and the standard discovery protocols devices answer (mDNS, SSDP/UPnP, NetBIOS). Nothing from the scan leaves your network.

Location

Android hides the Wi-Fi network name, the access point and the list of nearby networks from any app without location permission, so NetHealth asks for it to analyse Wi-Fi. It is optional: the "Scan nearby Wi-Fi networks" setting turns the request and the scans off, and the rest of the app keeps working. The connection monitor keeps that access while the screen is off so it can notice roaming between access points. The Route map uses the device's last known location to mark "you" only if location permission is already granted. Location is used on the device only and is never sent anywhere.

What is stored on your device

Reports, logs, the history of past checks, your settings, the devices seen on each network (their names and hardware addresses, so a new device can be marked as new) and simple notes per network (for example whether a sign-in page was ever seen there). All of it lives in the app's private storage, is never uploaded, and is removed when you uninstall the app. Reports > Clean up deletes reports and logs at any time.

Sharing

A report or log leaves your device only when you tap Share and pick where it goes. Reports can contain your network's details — addresses, the provider, Wi-Fi names — so look before you send one.

When the app works in the background

Only when you ask it to: the connection monitor and the two-device measurement run as a foreground service with a notification for as long as you leave them running, and the optional scheduled check (off by default, Reports > Check on a schedule) runs the checks — without the speed tests — every few hours and adds the result to the history on your device.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state — to run the tests.
  • Location / nearby Wi-Fi devices — required by Android to read Wi-Fi names and scan results (see above).
  • Wi-Fi state and multicast — to read the Wi-Fi link and to find other devices on your network.
  • Notifications, foreground service, wake lock — so a long monitor run keeps going with the screen off.
  • Battery optimisation — the monitor may ask to run unrestricted, so Android does not freeze it mid-run.
  • Run at start-up — only so a scheduled check you switched on survives a restart.

Children

NetHealth is a general-audience network utility. Because it collects no personal information, it does not knowingly collect data from children or anyone else.

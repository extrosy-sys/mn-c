Privacy Policy — WireVitals

App published by Extrosy · Last updated: 7 October 2026

The short version: WireVitals has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and Extrosy runs no servers — so we receive nothing from you. To test your connection the app has to contact public test servers, which see your internet address the way any website does. Your results, reports and logs stay on your device unless you choose to share them. If you buy WireVitals Pro, Google Play handles the purchase.

What we collect

Nothing. WireVitals does not send any information to Extrosy. There is no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics and no crash-reporting service. Besides Google's standard Android libraries (AndroidX and Material Components) the app contains one more Google component: the Google Play Billing Library, which offers WireVitals Pro.

Buying WireVitals Pro

WireVitals Pro is a one-time purchase through Google Play, which takes the payment under your Google account and under Google's own terms and privacy policy. The app never sees your payment details: it asks Google Play only for the price and whether your account owns Pro, and remembers the answer on your device. Extrosy sees only the order records Google Play gives every seller, and uses them only for refunds and support. The billing library may also send Google diagnostic information about its own working, as Google's policies describe.

What the app sends over the internet, and to whom

A network health checker cannot work offline: to measure your connection it has to talk to servers on the internet. Every server it contacts sees your device's public internet address and the request itself, exactly as any website you visit does; what those services do with that is covered by their own privacy policies. WireVitals sends them no personal information beyond what the test needs, as listed here.

Location

Android hides the Wi-Fi network name, the access point and the list of nearby networks from any app without location permission, so WireVitals asks for it to analyse Wi-Fi. It is optional: the "Scan nearby Wi-Fi networks" setting turns the request and the scans off, and the rest of the app keeps working. The connection monitor keeps that access while the screen is off so it can notice roaming between access points. The Route map uses the device's last known location to mark "you" only if location permission is already granted. Location is used on the device only and is never sent anywhere.

What is stored on your device

Reports, logs, the history of past checks, your settings, the devices seen on each network (their names and hardware addresses, so a new device can be marked as new), simple notes per network (for example whether a sign-in page was ever seen there), the notes you add to a monitor run, the speeds you pay for if you enter them for the evidence report, and whether you own Pro. All of it lives in the app's private storage, is never uploaded, and is removed when you uninstall the app. Reports > Clean up deletes reports and logs at any time.

Sharing

A report or log leaves your device only when you tap Share and pick where it goes. Reports can contain your network's details — addresses, the provider, Wi-Fi names — so look before you send one.

When the app works in the background

Only when you ask it to: the connection monitor and the two-device measurement run as a foreground service with a notification for as long as you leave them running, and the optional scheduled check (off by default, Reports > Check on a schedule) runs the checks every few hours and adds the result to the history on your device - without the speed tests unless you switch them on in Settings, and never on a metered network. With alerts on, the monitor posts a notification when the connection drops and when it comes back. The home-screen widget and the quick-settings tile run a check only when you tap them.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state — to run the tests.
  • Location / nearby Wi-Fi devices — required by Android to read Wi-Fi names and scan results (see above).
  • Wi-Fi state and multicast — to read the Wi-Fi link and to find other devices on your network.
  • Notifications, foreground service, wake lock — so a long monitor run keeps going with the screen off, and for the monitor's alerts.
  • Usage access (optional) — only for Tools > Network use by app: Android's own per-app data counts are read on the device and never sent anywhere. Android asks you to allow it, and you can take it back in its settings at any time.
  • Google Play billing — to offer WireVitals Pro through Google Play.
  • Battery optimisation — the monitor may ask to run unrestricted, so Android does not freeze it mid-run.
  • Run at start-up — only so a scheduled check you switched on survives a restart.

Children

WireVitals is a general-audience network utility. Because it collects no personal information, it does not knowingly collect data from children or anyone else.
